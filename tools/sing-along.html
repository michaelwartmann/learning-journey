What is jianpu 简谱?

简谱 jiǎnpǔ, "simple notation", is how most people in China read music: in school songbooks, karaoke sheets and church hymnals. Instead of notes on five lines, it writes the steps of the scale as numbers.

  • 1 2 3 4 5 6 7 = do re mi fa sol la ti. You sing "do" for 1, or the number itself: yāo/yī, èr, sān…
  • 1 = F at the top says which key "do" is. Change it and the same numbers sing higher or lower.
  • A dot above a number = one octave higher, a dot below = one octave lower.
  • A plain number lasts one beat. Underlined = half a beat, two lines = a quarter beat.
  • – after a note holds it one more beat. A dot after a number makes it half as long again.
  • 0 is a rest, | a bar line.

So the start of "Twinkle, Twinkle" is 1 1 5 5 | 6 6 5 – in China, and every Chinese friend can sing it from that line.
